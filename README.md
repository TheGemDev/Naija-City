# Lagos Life Open 🫡

> War on Nigerian developer's community hypocrasy

## The Problem Is Not Copying. The Problem Is Your Mentality.

Some Nigerians genuinely believe that once someone builds a product, everyone else should stay away from that entire category. Someone builds a life simulation game, and suddenly, anyone else who dares to build one is a thief, a copycat, or someone praying for the original creator's downfall.

**What exactly is wrong with you people?**

Who told you that being the first to execute an idea gives you exclusive ownership of that idea forever?

Here is something some people in this conversation desperately need to understand:

> **Being first gives you an opportunity to establish yourself in a market. It does not give you ownership of that market.**

If your product is good, you have every reason to keep building it, improving it, and growing your audience. But you cannot reasonably expect everyone else to abandon an entire product category simply because you entered it first.

You can respect a creator's work without believing that nobody else should be allowed to compete with them. You can support Lagos Life and still believe Naija City has a right to exist.

**The existence of competition is not evidence of wrongdoing. And the success of one product is not a reason to prevent another from being built.**

Lagos Life did not invent life simulation games. The Sims existed long before it. TikTok did not invent short-form video. ChatGPT did not invent artificial intelligence. Yet, when someone builds something in an existing category, improves on an existing concept, or targets a different audience, some of you act as though innovation itself has become a crime.

You cannot spend your entire life using products built on existing ideas, then turn around and condemn someone else for doing the same thing.

Instagram has Reels. YouTube has Shorts. Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek, and countless other AI products compete in a space where ChatGPT has become a household name.

Did the existence of ChatGPT mean nobody else should build an AI assistant? Should every company that wants to build one simply pack up because someone got there first?

Of course not.

**THAT IS NOT HOW COMPETITION WORKS.**

## Nobody Owes You a Monopoly

Building a product does not give you ownership of an entire market.

You can build something first, establish an audience, develop a community, and create a successful business. You deserve recognition for that work.

But you do not automatically acquire the right to dictate who else can enter the same space.

Someone can build a similar product without wanting you to fail. Someone can identify the same opportunity without conspiring against you. Someone can even build a better product without that achievement being an attack on yours.

And yes, someone else's success does not automatically mean yours must suffer.

**Two products can coexist. Two developers can compete. Two ideas can share similarities without being identical implementations.**

Competition can encourage better features, improved user experiences, new approaches, and products that serve users in ways existing alternatives do not.

Instead of asking why someone else is building in your space, perhaps the more productive question is what you can do to make your own product better.

If your product is genuinely good, competition gives you another reason to keep improving it. It does not invalidate everything you have already achieved.

**Being first is an advantage. It is not a lifetime entitlement to an uncontested market.**

## The Obsession With "Copying"

Apparently, in the Nigerian tech space, building something similar to an existing product is enough to trigger an entire investigation into your originality.

Nobody wants to examine the implementation, the features, the architecture, the user experience, or the problems being solved.

They see a similar concept and immediately reach for the word "copying."

But copying an idea, copying an implementation, stealing source code, and infringing intellectual property are not automatically the same thing.

These distinctions matter.

If someone steals your proprietary source code, copies protected assets without permission, misuses your branding, or infringes your intellectual property, you have legitimate grounds to challenge that conduct.

But if someone independently builds a life simulation game because they see an opportunity in that market, what exactly have they stolen merely by entering the category?

**You cannot claim that someone has stolen your entire idea simply because they have decided to compete with you.**

An idea is not a lifetime monopoly.

And being first does not make you immune to competition.

### Let's Be Clear: Not Everything Is Fair Game

Before anyone deliberately misrepresents this argument, let me make something clear.

This is not an argument that developers should be allowed to steal source code, copy proprietary assets, impersonate existing brands, or ignore intellectual property rights.

Those are separate issues.

There is a difference between independently developing a competing product and unlawfully taking another developer's protected work. Whether a particular implementation infringes someone's rights depends on the actual facts and applicable law.

**Competition is not a free pass to steal. But accusations of copying are not a free pass to prohibit competition either.**

If you believe someone has stolen your code or infringed your intellectual property, identify what was taken and explain the basis of your claim.

Simply pointing out that two products belong to the same category does not establish that theft has occurred.

A life simulation game does not become stolen property merely because another life simulation game already exists.

That distinction should not be difficult to understand.

## This Is Not an Attack on Lagos Life

Let me make this abundantly clear.

**I LOVE LAGOS LIFE.**

This is not an attack on Lagos Life, its creator, or the work that has gone into building the game. I can appreciate what someone has created while disagreeing completely with the mentality that other people should not be allowed to build in the same space.

Lagos Life has its own identity, community, features, and development journey. Its success does not need to be diminished for another life simulation game to exist.

The issue is the people who have decided that competition is equivalent to sabotage, that similar ideas automatically constitute theft, and that another developer's attempt to build something is somehow an attempt to deprive the original creator of their success.

**THIS IS AN ATTACK ON THAT MENTALITY.**

Not on Lagos Life.

Not on its creator.

On the ridiculous idea that the moment something starts succeeding, everyone else must stand back and watch instead of exploring the opportunity for themselves.

You can love Lagos Life and still acknowledge that other developers have the right to build competing products.

You can respect its creator without treating every alternative as an enemy.

You can support the original game without demanding that everyone else abandon the category.

These positions are not contradictory.

**Supporting one developer does not require you to oppose every other developer.**

If Lagos Life is your preferred game, play it. Support it. Recommend it. Help its creator improve it.

But do not confuse your loyalty to a product with an obligation for everyone else to stop building.

## Business Does Not Operate on Sympathy

Nobody owes you a market free from competition.

Nobody is obligated to abandon an opportunity because you discovered it first.

And nobody should be expected to stop building simply because a vocal section of your audience has decided that the existence of alternatives is offensive.

In business, people compete through product quality, execution, distribution, marketing, customer experience, and innovation.

Sometimes the original product wins.

Sometimes a competitor wins.

Sometimes both succeed by serving different audiences.

And sometimes both fail.

**THAT IS LIFE.**

If you build a good product, keep improving it, understand your audience, and execute your vision properly, you give yourself a chance to succeed.

But if your entire strategy depends on nobody else being allowed to build something similar, you are not defending innovation. You are defending a monopoly over an idea you do not exclusively own.

The market does not owe you sympathy simply because you arrived first.

Nobody is obligated to protect you from competition just because you invested time, money, or effort into your product.

Your investment deserves respect. Your work deserves to be evaluated on its merits. But neither automatically gives you exclusive control over an entire product category.

**Build better. Innovate harder. Compete.**

Stop expecting the world to stand still because you were the first person to do something.

## Criticism of Competition Is Not Criticism of Execution

There is another distinction that seems to be getting lost in this conversation.

People are allowed to criticise Naija City.

You can argue that its features are inadequate, its implementation is poor, its user experience needs work, or its execution does not distinguish it enough from existing alternatives.

Those are legitimate criticisms of a product, and I am not asking anyone to pretend otherwise.

You can even argue that Lagos Life is the better game. That is your opinion, and you are entitled to it.

But none of those arguments automatically establishes that Naija City should not exist.

**Criticising how a product is built is different from arguing that nobody should be allowed to build it in the first place.**

If Naija City is not good enough, let users decide that through their experience with the game.

If Lagos Life offers a better experience, let it compete on that basis.

If Naija City has shortcomings, those shortcomings are mine to address as its developer.

But declaring a project illegitimate simply because another product in the same category already exists is not a meaningful criticism of its execution.

It is opposition to competition itself.

And if you believe in innovation, you should understand why those are two very different positions.

## Why This Repository Exists

This repository exists to make a point.

Naija City is an independent life simulation project, built because there is an opportunity to explore this category of games.

Making this project open source is also a statement about how I believe software development should work: people should be able to examine implementations, learn from them, build upon their own ideas, and compete through what they create.

I am not asking anyone to abandon Lagos Life.

I am not asking anyone to stop supporting its creator.

I am not asking anyone to declare Naija City the better game.

I am simply refusing to accept that building a competing product is inherently wrong.

If you believe Lagos Life is the better game, play Lagos Life.

If you believe Naija City is the better game, play Naija City.

If you believe neither is good enough, build your own.

**BUT STOP ACTING LIKE COMPETITION IS A CRIME.**

You are free to support whichever product you prefer. You are free to criticise my implementation, question my decisions, and explain why you prefer another game.

What you are not entitled to is the expectation that other developers should abandon an entire category simply because you already have a favourite.

The goal is to build, learn, improve, and compete.

Not to beg for permission.

Not to seek sympathy.

Not to wait for everyone to approve.

**MAY THE BEST IMPLEMENTATION WIN. 🫡**
